Every session shows as a terminal pane: a shell, a dev server, or an
agent's own screen. The app draws it with Ghostty's terminal core compiled
to WebAssembly (ghostty-web), or with xterm.js if that can't start, and
attaches it to the session's tmux on the box through the laptop agent. A
pane stays attached while its tab is in the background; if the link drops,
it reattaches on its own and the box redraws the screen.

## Settings

**Settings → Terminal** applies to every terminal pane, and open ones
update as you change it:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| Font, Size, Line height | ⌘+ and ⌘− with a terminal focused change the size; ⌘0 resets it. |
| Cursor | Block, bar or underline, blinking or not. |
| Scrollback | Lines kept in the app. The box's tmux keeps 50,000 more, redrawn when you reattach. |
| Predict typing | Adaptive, Always or Never: see below. |
| Copy on select | Selecting text copies it. |
| Renderer | Ghostty or xterm.js. If Ghostty can't start, the app says why here. |

## Typing on a slow link

A box reached through a relay can be 80 ms to 2 s away, and a terminal only
shows a key once the box has echoed it back. To keep typing from feeling
broken, Shipyard predicts the echo the way [Mosh](https://mosh.org) does:
what you type shows at once, underlined, and the underline goes when the
box's own echo arrives and agrees.

- **When.** Each terminal measures the time from a keystroke to its echo.
  With **Adaptive** (the default), predictions show once that is over about
  60 ms, so a nearby box looks exactly as before. **Always** shows them on
  any link; **Never** turns them off.
- **What is predicted.** Characters typed at the cursor, Backspace, and
  Left and Right along the line you are typing.
- **What isn't.** Enter, Tab, Up and Down, Ctrl keys and pastes do whatever
  the program decides, so they are never predicted. After one, the next key
  waits for its own echo; once that comes back, the keys after it show at
  once again.
- **The box always wins.** Predictions are drawn over the terminal, never
  written into it. If the box draws something else where a prediction was
  (a job finishing, a completion menu, output from elsewhere), every
  prediction still waiting is taken back and the box's screen shows as it
  is. Prediction then waits until the box confirms one again.
- **Passwords.** A password prompt doesn't echo, so its predictions are
  never confirmed and never show.
- **Full-screen programs.** In vim, less and the like, typing is predicted
  only once the program has echoed a key where it was typed, and keys that
  move around (vim's normal mode, less's scrolling) never show.
- **Where.** In shells and other terminals you type in. An agent's own
  screen (Claude Code, Codex), the chat's terminal and a scrolled-back
  terminal get none.

Both renderers show predictions. Nothing about them animates, so Reduce
motion has nothing to change.
