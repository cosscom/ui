Releases are on [GitHub](https://github.com/cosscom/shipyard/releases).
The Mac app [updates itself](/docs/getting-started/install#updates); a box
upgrades with `berth upgrade BOX`, or by running the
[install command](/docs/getting-started/add-a-box#with-the-install-command)
again.

## Unreleased
- The website's header is now coss.com's: "coss.com shipyard", with Docs,
  Download, GitHub and its stars, and "Close the laptop" as an icon, and coss.com's
  lines down the sides of the page and across the top of each chapter.
- **Review no longer keeps a box busy while an item is open.** With an item
  open, Review read the box's inbox and the item's diff again every couple of
  seconds for as long as it showed, each read setting off the next. It now
  reads them once, and again only when something changes.
- **Make a worktree without waiting for its setup.** `worktree new --no-setup`
  creates it at once, without the setup script or autostart services, and
  `worktree setup LOC/NAME [--wait]` runs them later.
- **Leadline: what your code did in development, as tabs on a worktree.** A
  new plugin in **Settings → Plugins → Browse** adds **Queries**,
  **Traces**, **Logs** and **Endpoints** to a worktree: every database
  query grouped by shape with its N+1s flagged, each request as a
  waterfall with its SQL and logs, and per-route latency and queries per
  request. **Send to the agent** writes a slow query, an N+1 or a failing
  endpoint up for the worktree's agent, and shows you the text before it
  goes. It is off until you turn it on for a box, which installs a small
  program there (`berth-leadline`) after asking; what it records stays on
  that box, for 72 hours. Queries to a Postgres on the box are recorded
  with no changes to your app; traces and logs need an OpenTelemetry SDK
  in it. A plugin that carries a program like this says so, by the
  program's name, before you allow it and in its row in Settings →
  Plugins. See [Leadline](/docs/guides/leadline); plugin authors get
  `berth.helper` and a `Chart` component in the
  [plugin SDK](/docs/reference/plugin-sdk#helper).
- **Plugins can be installed from the app.** Settings → Plugins has a new
  **Browse** list of plugins that aren't built in, starting with Hello
  ports. Install puts one under Installed, off: it runs only once you allow
  it, like any plugin of your own, and after an update Shipyard asks again.
  Remove deletes one you installed this way. To list a plugin of yours, see
  [publish it to the listing](/docs/guides/plugins#publish-it-to-the-listing).
- **Shipyard has its own mark.** The B from when the app was called Berth is
  gone: the icon is now a hull seen bow-on, resting on its keel blocks, with
  the amber lamp lit inside. It is on the app and its dock icon, the phone
  app, the site and the docs.
- **Linear issues in Shipyard.** Paste a personal API key in **Settings →
  Integrations**, and **Issues** gets a Linear side beside GitHub: what is
  assigned to you or a team's open issues, each with its description and
  comments, and **Start an agent** on any of them, in the project you
  choose, on the branch Linear suggests for the issue. A row then shows how
  its agent is doing. Home has a **Linear** widget for what is assigned to
  you. The key stays in a file only you can read on this computer: the
  laptop agent makes every call to Linear, and the app, plugins and boxes
  never see it. See [Linear](/docs/guides/linear).
- **`worktree new` can check out a pull request.** Pass `--pr N` with the
  pull request's head branch as `--branch`, and the worktree starts on that
  branch as origin has it. A pull request from a fork is fetched from
  `pull/N/head`, or from the ref you give with `--ref`.
- **Links in a terminal open with ⌘-click, and say so.** A link used to
  underline and show the pointing hand on a plain hover, where a click did
  nothing, and links a program made with its own text (OSC 8) never opened
  at all. Now resting on a link shows "⌘-click to open", the hand appears
  while ⌘ is held, and ⌘-click opens the address in your browser, OSC 8
  links included (Ctrl-click in the Linux app). A long link that wraps
  over several rows, such as Codex's ChatGPT sign-in link, opens whole
  from any of its rows; before, it couldn't be clicked at all.
- **A home widget's ⋯ menu opens again.** It broke the home screen with
  "Base UI error #31" instead of showing its Size and Refresh options.
- **A Browser tab on a port with nothing listening no longer crashes the
  app.** Opening `http://localhost:3000` before its dev server started closed
  Shipyard half a second later, and again on every launch after, since the
  tab came back.
- **The laptop agent is lighter per request.** Proxied pages, port forwards
  and calls to your boxes allocate a fraction of what they did, and the kits
  and team suggestions ask all your boxes at once.
- **Shipyard opens faster.** It now loads four files before it first paints
  instead of about three hundred, and settings, dialogs and the browser pane
  load just after start. In our tests, the window drew in about a fifth of the
  time.
- **Chats keep up while an agent writes.** A streamed reply costs about a
  third of the work it did on the page, and typing in a chat, Home's composer
  or the sidebar's menus stays quick with hundreds of worktrees.
- **The app asks the agent and your boxes less.** An idle chat makes about
  half the requests it did, starting reads every box once, and a box's large
  answers cross the link to your laptop about ten times smaller.
- **berthd uses less of a box.** Following a chat, a reply as it streams and
  the agents' screens runs fewer tmux commands and less CPU; a chat's own
  poll takes about a quarter of what it did.
  Opening a long conversation uses a tenth of the memory, and a cache that grew
  with every conversation now stays small.
- **The Mac app is 20% smaller.**

## v0.3.13

Most of this needs each box's new `berthd`: use **Update all** in the status
bar, or `berth upgrade BOX`.

- **The Review in Shipyard button is for UI work.** berthd adds it only to a
  PR whose files change something a browser shows, agents add it only for
  such changes, and the button is the words alone, without a mark.

- **Shipyard notices when your team has a setup.** If you added a project
  from `acme` yourself and acme publishes a [team setup](/docs/guides/team-setup),
  Shipyard says so quietly: a small icon on the project's row and a line in
  its menu, a line in Project settings saying what it sets up for the repo,
  a card on Home once, and **Team setup for acme** in ⌘K. **Review…** opens
  the setup with your project used as it is; nothing runs until you choose.
  **Not now** and **Don't suggest again for acme** put it away. The laptop
  checks only your projects' owners, with your own `gh`, at most daily (weekly
  for a person's account), and never offline. The project menu also has
  **Copy team setup link**. See
  [how Shipyard notices your team's setup](/docs/guides/team-setup#how-shipyard-notices-your-teams-setup).

- **Just the kit from a team setup.** **Use acme's kit for this project…**
  applies only a project's kit from `acme/.berth` to the project you have: no
  box steps, sudo, clone or 1Password. The sheet lists the tools the kit needs
  and whether the box has them; the shared keys and the first-time setup are
  each an opt-in. The kit then follows the team's newer commits as an update
  to review. See [just the kit](/docs/guides/team-setup#just-the-kit).

- **Team setup uses the clones you already have.** Before cloning a repo,
  Shipyard looks for one on the box: a project you added already, or a
  clone in `~/code`, `~/work`, `~/src` and the like (matched by its origin,
  reading nothing else). The page shows "Found ~/work/acme-web … · Use this
  / Clone a fresh copy", and using it changes nothing in it: no checkout,
  stash, reset or pull, and your `.env` and uncommitted changes stay as
  they are. Its existing worktrees show in the sidebar and are set up the
  first time you open each. `berth team setup … --use web=PATH` does the
  same from the command line. See
  [repositories you already cloned](/docs/guides/team-setup#repositories-you-already-cloned).

- **Review links can open logged in, at a page.** Add `&as=pro@acme.test&path=/event-types`
  to a review link (or `--as` and `--path` to `berth review`), and the
  review opens in its Browser tab logged in as that dev user, at that page,
  when the project's login lists them; otherwise it opens without logging
  in, and the sheet says why. **Copy review link as…** in the worktree menu
  makes one. See [review a PR](/docs/guides/review-a-pr#opening-logged-in-at-a-page).

- **A Review in Shipyard button on pull requests.** GitHub drops
  `berth://` links from PR descriptions, so a review link is now a button
  that goes through `https://berthd.app/review`, a small page that checks
  the link and opens the review in Shipyard (with a link to the PR on
  GitHub, and to install Shipyard). Agents add it to the PRs they open,
  logged in as one of the project's dev users and at the page their change
  affects (`berthd login users` lists them). Turn on `"review_button"` for a
  project (its `.berth/config.json`, Project settings, or the team's
  `team.json`) and berthd adds it to each new PR from the project's
  worktrees by itself, once, without touching the rest of the description;
  never to a fork's PR or one from outside the org.
- **Review a PR in one click, on your own box.** A review link,
  `berth://review?repo=acme/shop&pr=42`, or `berth review acme/shop#42`,
  opens a review sheet: the PR, its author, the exact commit, the box, what
  will run there, which keys it gets, and what in the PR changes setup.
  **Review** makes a worktree at that commit, set up by your team's kit,
  and opens it with the dev server in a Browser tab and the Diff beside it.
  Only your team's projects, and only PRs by members and collaborators from
  the repository itself; never a fork's. The PR's own `.berth/` never runs,
  the review never gets your own keys, and it cleans itself up when the PR
  is merged or closed, or after a week unused. **Copy review link** is in
  every worktree's menu whose branch has a PR. See
  [review a PR](/docs/guides/review-a-pr).
- **Open a worktree logged in as a dev user.** A kit, or a project's
  config, can name a login script and the users it logs in (`"login":
  {"script", "users", "any"}`). The Browser tab's **Log in as…** menu and ⌘K
  list them; picking one runs the script against that worktree's own dev
  server and database and sets its session for that worktree's address
  alone. Agents get the same with `berthd browser open --as EMAIL`, and
  `berthd shots compare --as EMAIL` shoots both sides logged in. A link
  works too: `http://WT.LOC.BOX.localhost:1377/__berth/login?as=EMAIL&next=/path`.
  See [kits](/docs/guides/kits#personas-logging-in-as-a-dev-user).

## v0.3.12

- **The Linux alpha's downloads.** v0.3.11's Linux build stopped before it
  began (Ubuntu 22.04, which it builds on for older systems, has no
  `minisign`), so v0.3.11 shipped for the Mac only. This release carries the
  AppImage and `.deb` for x86-64 and adds Linux to the update feed; see
  [Linux (alpha)](/docs/getting-started/install#linux-alpha).

- **Install with Homebrew.** `brew tap cosscom/shipyard https://github.com/cosscom/shipyard`,
  then `brew install --cask cosscom/shipyard/shipyard`, installs the Mac app
  and puts `berth` on your `PATH`.

- **Handed-off worktrees nest under the one they came from.** When an
  agent starts a task, or hands its work off, the new worktree shows under
  the agent's own in the sidebar, folded behind a "1 child" pill. `berthd
  worktree new … --parent NAME` nests one by hand, such as a stacked branch
  under the branch it builds on.

- **GitHub flows see every worktree's PR.** One poll looks at no more than
  20 PRs, and it always started from the same worktree, so on a box with
  more worktrees than that the rest were never looked at: a merge, review or
  failed check there started nothing. Each poll now starts where the last
  one stopped. Merged and closed PRs no longer take a turn every poll
  either; a flow looks at them again once an hour, in case one is reopened.
  [GitHub triggers](/docs/guides/automations#on-github).

## v0.3.11

Most of this needs each box's new `berthd`: use **Update all** in the status
bar, or `berth upgrade BOX`. Agents keep running through the update.

- **More than one way to each box.** Shipyard can now reach a box over SSH
  and at other addresses as well as over Tailscale, and sends new requests
  over the fastest route that works. When the route in use stops answering,
  requests move to another within seconds; open terminals follow once it is
  clearly down, without closing, and keys typed meanwhile still arrive. A
  box you added over SSH gets its SSH route on its own. Settings › Boxes
  shows each box's routes with their latency ("via SSH, 24 ms · Tailscale,
  140 ms"), and turns them on or off; `berth boxes` shows the route in use
  and `berth doctor BOX` lists them all.
- **Sessions clean up after themselves.** Ending a session now stops
  everything it started, such as a repository's Playwright tests and their
  browsers or a dev server, even ones left running in the background after
  the agent was killed. On Linux with systemd each new session runs in a
  scope of its own, which also lets it outlive berthd as before; elsewhere
  Shipyard finds the session's processes by their tree (and, on Linux, the
  session's marker). Your own processes and tmux are never touched.
- **See and stop a box's browsers.** Click a box's memory meter in the
  status bar to see every Chromium, Chrome and Firefox on it, with who
  started it (Shipyard's agent browser, an agent-browser session, a
  session's tests, one left behind, or yours), its CPU, memory and age, and
  **Stop** for Shipyard's and its sessions'. The sessions using the most
  memory are below. Settings → Boxes shows the same, and agents see it with
  `berthd ps` and in `berthd doctor`.
- **A memory limit per session.** Settings → Boxes can hold each session on
  a box under a limit, such as 12 GB (Linux with systemd). A session near it
  is slowed down, never stopped, and its chat and tab say "using 11.8 GB,
  near its 12 GB limit".
- **A lighter agent browser.** It closes after 5 idle minutes instead of 10
  (agent-browser sessions too), keeps to two renderer processes, and caps a
  page's JavaScript heap at 512 MB, which saves about 35 MB per browser
  (`BERTH_BROWSER_JS_HEAP_MB` raises it).
- **A slow or relayed link to a box stays connected and says why.** A box
  reached through a Tailscale relay far away (80 ms, then 2 s) used to flip
  to offline and back: the agent called a box away after one health check
  with no answer, and checked it whenever a request had waited 5 s. Now a
  slow or unanswered check marks the box **slow**, not offline: requests to
  it still go through and its connection, with the terminals on it, is
  kept. Only a second failure in a row takes it away (a refused connection,
  a changed key or berthd stopping still does at once), so a box that
  really went shows Reconnecting in about 20 s. A request now waits 10 s
  before it has the box checked. The status bar and sidebar say "slow",
  quietly; **Settings › Boxes** shows the latency, why it's slow, and when
  Tailscale relays the box: "Relayed through Tailscale's New York server:
  no direct connection", with what fixes it. `berth doctor BOX` says so
  too, and the agent's log has a line for every change of a box's state,
  with why. [A relayed link](/docs/concepts/laptop-agent#a-relayed-link).
- **`berth ping` says a box's latency.** It printed one number that was
  connecting, TLS and a request together (about three round trips), so a box
  100 ms away read as 300 ms. It now says how long connecting took, then
  pings on the open connection, as the app uses it: `cal: 88ms 90ms 118ms
  (min 88ms, avg 99ms, max 118ms on the open connection)`. `-c N` sets how
  many.
- **Typing in a terminal keeps up on a slow link.** On a box 80 ms to 2 s
  away (a relayed Tailscale link), a shell showed each key only once the box
  had echoed it. Now what you type shows at once, underlined until the box's
  echo agrees, as in Mosh; anything the box draws differently takes every
  prediction back, and password prompts never show one. It turns on by
  itself when the round trip is over about 60 ms: **Settings → Terminal →
  Predict typing** has Always and Never too. See
  [Terminals](/docs/guides/terminals#typing-on-a-slow-link).
- **The Browser tab's address follows the page.** An app that changes pages
  on the client (Next.js, React Router: `history.pushState`) left the
  address bar on the first URL, since nothing loaded. It now shows where the
  page is, within half a second.
- **Every agent account gets Shipyard's hooks and skills.** A Claude Code or
  Codex session started on another account (the Usage plugin's accounts,
  which set `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` or `CODEX_HOME`) ran without Shipyard's
  hooks, so the box guessed from the screen when it was working, done or
  needed you, and without Shipyard's skills. `berthd integrations install`
  now puts them in every account folder (the default, those in
  `~/.berth/accounts`, and any the box or a project picks), **Add account…**
  puts them in the new one, and berthd adds them to an account a session
  starts on before its agent runs. `berthd integrations` and `berthd doctor`
  list them per account. See
  [Agent integrations](/docs/guides/agent-integrations#every-account).
- **A chat on another account reads its conversation.** An agent started on
  another Claude Code or Codex account (the Usage plugin's accounts, which
  set `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` or `CODEX_HOME`) wrote its conversation to that
  account's folder, and the box only looked in its own: the chat said "Its
  conversation can't be read here yet" and emptied after every message. The
  box now reads each session's conversation from the account it started on.
- **Berth is now Shipyard.** A new name, nothing else: the same app, the
  same CLI and the same boxes, with your data where it was. The commands are
  still `berth` and `berthd`, and their files (`~/.berth`, a repository's
  `.berth/`, `BERTH_*` variables, `berth://` links) keep their names for now.
  A new download is `Shipyard.app`; an installed `Berth.app` updates in
  place and keeps its folder name. Releases before this one went out as
  Berth.
- **Shipyard for Linux, an alpha.** The desktop app now ships for x86-64
  Linux as an AppImage, which updates itself from the same feed as the Mac
  app, and as a .deb. It's an alpha: less tested than the Mac app, and its
  sidebar and **Settings → About** say so, with **Report a Linux bug** in
  About. Ctrl stands in for ⌘ (in a terminal, add Shift: Ctrl+Shift+T, and
  Ctrl+Shift+C and V copy and paste); there's no menu bar; **Start at
  login** makes the agent a systemd user service. arm64 isn't built yet.
  [Linux (alpha)](/docs/getting-started/install#linux-alpha).

## v0.3.10

- **What's new, once after an update.** The Mac app updates itself
  quietly, so its new things went unseen. After an update, a note at the
  foot of the sidebar says what's new (with the sidebar folded, the card
  opens instead). The card shows the release's highlights one at a time,
  each with a small picture of it in the app and **Show me**, which goes
  there: the worktree's artifacts board, a visual diff, a Browser tab with
  its console open, or renaming a worktree; the rest show their keys or
  their guide. ← and → page through it, Esc closes it. A new install
  doesn't get it, and × on the note puts it away for good. **Settings →
  About → What's new** and ⌘K open it again.
  [Updates](/docs/getting-started/install#updates).
- **A team setup's repos are ready when it says so.** Each repo's
  first-time setup (its init) runs in a terminal of its own on the box,
  which the Team setup page never showed. An init that stopped at a question,
  such as corepack's "Do you want to continue? [Y/n]" before it downloads
  yarn, left the repo setting up until opening its main checkout happened to
  show that terminal; answering there finished the setup, which is why
  worktrees only worked after **Open main checkout**. Now corepack doesn't
  ask (in inits and worktree setup scripts). A repo's row shows its init's
  last line and a **Terminal** button. When the init asks anything, the row
  says **waiting for you**, the status card names the repo, and **Answer in
  its terminal** opens it. **Open main checkout** is a way to open the repo
  again, not a step.
  [Progress, failure and Retry](/docs/guides/team-setup#progress-failure-and-retry).
- **An agent CLI you installed with npm is found.** Claude Code installed
  with `npm i -g @anthropic-ai/claude-code` under nvm (or fnm, volta, bun,
  pnpm, a custom npm prefix or Homebrew) wasn't picked up on a new box,
  where the one **Add agents…** installs was: berthd runs as a service, and
  only your interactive shell's `~/.bashrc` knows where nvm put it. berthd
  now asks your shell as a terminal starts it (`$SHELL -lic`, reading only
  its answer, past any banner, and giving up after ten seconds), then looks
  in the folders those installers use. With both installs, the one your
  shell finds first is used. Agents start with it and the `PATH` it was
  found with, so its `node` is found too. `berthd doctor` and **Settings →
  Boxes** say which one, such as `Claude Code 2.1.3 · ~/.nvm/…/bin/claude
  (npm)`, with **Look again** for one installed since; `berthd agents
  install` no longer installs a second copy, and `berthd integrations
  install` finds it the same way.
  [Agent CLIs you installed yourself](/docs/getting-started/add-a-box#agent-clis-you-installed-yourself).
- **The agent's browser has the size the agent chooses.** It opens at
  1920×1080 now (it was 1280×800), and an agent sets another with
  `berthd browser resize 390x844 --scale 3`, a preset (`phone`,
  `phone-max` and `tablet` as in Preview, `laptop`, `desktop`), or
  `open --size … --scale …`. The size is kept for the worktree until it
  changes, a running browser changes at once, and widths from 320 to 3840,
  heights from 240 to 2160 and scales from 1 to 3 are allowed, with an error
  that says the range otherwise. `open`, `status`, `list`, `doctor` and
  `GET /v1/browser/health` say the size; `shot` follows it (`--native` for
  the page's own pixels). In the app, **Agent's view** says the size and the
  zoom it shows the page at, and fits the page to the pane without enlarging
  it past its own size, so a 2x page stays sharp. The `berth-browser` skill
  tells agents to check a phone size too. [Agent browser](/docs/guides/agent-browser#size)
- Files an agent's browser downloads land in the worktree's
  `.berth/browser/`, as the guide says, rather than the folder berthd
  started in.

- **A slow or dropping link to a box says so, and recovers by itself.**
  Tested against boxes 200–900 ms away, links with jitter, a bandwidth cap,
  stalls, resets, outages of 10 s, 60 s and 5 minutes, and a laptop asleep.
  A box that drops off now reads **Reconnecting to devl… Next try in 6s**
  in its terminals, chats and a team setup in progress, with **Try now**
  (which checks the box at once; it used to only re-read what the agent
  already knew). The agent tries an away box after about 2 s, then 4 and
  every 8 s, each with jitter (never less often than before), checks a box
  at once when a request to it has had no answer for 5 s, and answers
  requests for an away box at once rather than after a 15 s dial,
  so ⌘P, Files and the rest stop spinning. Reads now give up after 30 s
  with "devl isn't answering" instead of spinning on, and ⌘P says why in
  words and searches again when the box is back. Typing into a terminal
  that gets nothing back for 4 s shows **Waiting for devl to answer…**. A
  chat message sent again after a dropped answer carries the same
  idempotency key, so it is typed once, not twice. The app's event stream
  to the agent reconnects with jittered backoff and is dropped and opened
  again after 45 s of silence (a laptop that slept), and refreshes that
  land together after a reconnect run as one instead of piling up.
  [Slow and dropping networks](/docs/concepts/laptop-agent#slow-and-dropping-networks).
- **Berth works from the keyboard and with a screen reader.** Under a
  chat, its agent's terminal no longer takes Tab and keeps it. Closing
  ⌘K, ⌘P or a rename gives the keyboard back instead of dropping it;
  a file opened from ⌘P takes the keyboard, as do the Files panel (⌘⇧E)
  and the console drawer (⌘⌥I), whose tabs switch with the arrows and
  whose edge sizes with ↑ and ↓. Shift-F10 opens a tab's menu, ← and →
  fold a project in the sidebar, answering a permission or a question
  keeps the keyboard, and focus rings show in every theme
  (3:1, and muted text 4.5:1 on menus and highlighted rows: 22 themes
  moved a shade; code in the File tab and in chats reads at 4.5:1 too,
  each colour a shade darker or lighter). Screen readers hear who said
  each message in a chat,
  and hear an agent that finishes, needs you or fails, alerts first.
  ⌘K now lists every shortcut's action, each part of Settings, Review,
  a Preview tab, Rename this worktree and Do not disturb; the shortcuts
  sheet adds F2, ⌘F, ↵, esc, ← → and ⇧F10. With Reduce motion on in
  macOS, spinners, shimmer and pulses hold still and nothing slides.
- **Artifacts: charts, tables, diagrams and small pages from your agents,
  live.** An agent writes a `berth.chart` JSON, a CSV, Mermaid, Markdown or
  one HTML file and runs `berthd artifact add`; its chat shows a card with a
  live thumbnail and the headline its data gives ("/search 1,240 → 410 ms
  (−67%)", "3 failed of 16"). Open it as a tab, or ⌘-click to open it
  beside the chat; the tab has a Live pill that pulses when the agent
  rewrites the file, its versions (the first and the newest eight) and its
  source. **N artifacts** under the reply box, or the grid button in the
  worktree's toolbar, opens the worktree's board, filtered by Charts,
  Tables, Diagrams, Pages and Notes. Charts are drawn with
  [bklit UI](https://ui.bklit.com) in your theme, in all 23; a page runs on
  its own origin with no network and no access to Berth. The box keeps
  them outside the worktree, checks each version, and refuses one that
  looks like a secret. The `berth-artifacts` skill teaches Claude Code and
  Codex when and how (`berthd integrations install`). The claude.ai chip
  now reads **N published**. [Artifacts](/docs/guides/artifacts).

- **Visual diffs: what a change did to your pages.** After a UI change, an
  agent runs `berthd shots compare`: the worktree's pages and main's are
  shot at 375, 768 and 1280 pixels wide in a short-lived Chromium of their
  own (reduced motion, animations settled, fonts and images loaded, lazy
  content scrolled in, UTC), diffed pixel by pixel with content that only
  moved counted as moved, and kept as an artifact in its chat, in about 5
  seconds for 6 pages × 3 sizes. The tab opens on the biggest change: a
  before/after **Slider**, **Side by side**, **Flicker** or **Onion skin**,
